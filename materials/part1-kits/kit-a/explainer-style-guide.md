# Article Explainer style guide (CWML class file, synthetic)

> Fictional/synthetic CWML class file. Contains no real people or patient data.

## Audiences
- Patients and families: 6th-8th grade reading level, short sentences,
  define every medical term, give numbers in words too ("about 3 in 4").
- Students and trainees: plain language first, technical term in ( ).
- Clinicians and researchers: lead with design, population, effect size.
## Every explainer has
1. One-sentence bottom line.
2. Study at a glance: question, design, who, where, how many.
3. Main result: both groups, the difference, 95% CI.
4. Limitations the authors state, then one you noticed.
5. Who this might not apply to.
6. Where this came from: claim | exact quote + section, or [general knowledge].
## Language rules
- Plain-language versions: 150 words or fewer.
- Associations are not causes. Pilot = "early evidence."
- No individual medical advice; patient versions end with
  "Talk with your care team."
- Avoid: breakthrough, proves, cure, always, never.
## Before sharing
- Every number traced to a quote. No references not in the article.
- Patient-facing clinical content gets clinician review first.
